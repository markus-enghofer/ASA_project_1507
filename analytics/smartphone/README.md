# Smartphone

## Data collection

An iPhone 3 was held by hand while driving with an auto-rikshaw in Mysuru for around 10 minutes. The road conditions varied a lot from paved main roads to bumpy paths. The Smartphone collected data using an accelerometer, gyroscope and GPS, which also provided an estimation of bearing and orientation. Sudden changes of the road surface texture as well as single anomalies like pothole or speedbreakers were recorded manually. The dataset is divided into three data frames. Each of them is one to four minutes.

## Preprocessing

As the data provided by Smartphone was distributed over five different csv-files, namely accelerometer.csv, gravity.csv, gyroscope.csv, location.csv and orientation.csv, the observations were synchronized using timestamps_synchronization.ipynb. To do so, all csv-files must be stored in the same folder that should be reachable by the path-string assigned to OUTPUT-DIR. The three frames that were recorded are entirely stored in sample1, sample2 and sample3, respectively. The timestamps of the accelerometer served as reference.
The columns of roll and pitch had to be swapped in the dataset. Apart from that, the data was coherent and the measurements of the accelerometer and the gyroscope were roughly compatible with the GPS data. The accurate measurements of the impact of gravity were used to calculate the horizontal area, whereas the exact determination of the driving direction with respect to the smartphone's coordinate system was challenging. The orientation of the phone relative to the vehicle was not calibrated in the beginning. Estimates of the roll and pitch direction were calculated in different ways to extract the angular velocity around the x-axis and to the y-axis with respect to the orientation of the vehicle. However, as the absolute value of the horizontal angular velocity turned out to be one of the best predictors for road anomalies, a precise distinction of x- and y-axis might not be necessary here. As the measurements of both linear acceleration and angular-velocity are very noisy and at the same time highly frequent, smoothing was done by replacing each value by the weighted mean of an odd number of consecutive values with respect to the normal distribution. For each covariate, the exact number of considered values and the standard deviation of the normal distribution were determined using the function find_optimal_parameters(), which maximises the sum of the ranges in the neighborhoods of recorded road anomalies divided by the standard deviation of the entire frame. As both vertical acceleration and angular velocity are heavily impacted by the driving speed of the vehicle, all parameters were divided by the speed estimates provided by GPS. In additional to that analysis that were performed in exploratory_analysis.ipynb, also the largest jerks, the differences between two values of consecutive observations, of all relevant parameters were examined in the file drastic_changes.ipynb.
For both exploratory_analysis.ipynb and drastic_changes.ipynb, only the variable "desktop_path" has to be adapted according to the location of the considered frame before running the code.

## Results

While no meaningful patterns were visible in the raw data plots, the range in the neighborhood of most of the recorded events was significantly higher than elsewhere for the modified parameters a_z_scaled, gx_scaled_rotated, g_scaled_abs. However, the goodness of fit was not as good as for the data obtained by the AgileX robot placed in the auto-rikshaw. Road sections that were reportedly in worse condition could be identified by higher local variance of vertical acceleration and angular velocity. The analysis of drastic_changes did not lead to meaningful results.
